Every week another platform announces a marketplace where AI agents bid on work and get paid. We measured the ones that exist. Markets are graded on settlement evidence — whether money demonstrably reached a worker — not on funding, design, roadmap, or announcement volume.

A number here is only as good as its provenance, so each one is marked: ● confirmed by settlement evidence, ○ claimed by the platform, — not publicly verifiable. A blank is never a zero.

03 — Where the money actually is

The paying work is not shaped like a task queue

Nearly every dollar of credibly claimable agent work traces back to a small number of institutions funding it directly. Open bidding boards, where agents compete for posted tasks, are where the money is not.

The comparison nobody draws

The market for humans doing AI-adjacent work — labelling, evaluation, expert data — runs at roughly $5.9B annualised across six companies, Mercor alone at $2B●. Agent-work marketplaces are at $25K–$50K cumulative○, self-reported. Demand for AI-related labour is enormous. Almost none of it is routed through an agent marketplace.

Agents do earn at real scale — Sierra at roughly $200M ARR, priced per successful resolution●. Sold as enterprise contracts with an accountable vendor. Zero marketplace in between.

04 — Before you build one

The category is oversupplied with sellers and starved of buyers

Four markets in the census failed for the same structural reason: supply arrived, demand never did. Any new entrant inherits this condition on day one, and inherits it worse, because the sellers are already listed somewhere else.

The take rate is already anchored at 3%

AWS Marketplace charges 3% to distribute an agent, and cut professional services to 0.5% in June 2026. Microsoft charges 3%, down from 20% in 2021.● Upwork sustains 19.4%● because it underwrites trust, escrow and disputes for unreliable human supply. A new entrant inherits the 3% anchor and none of the 20% justifications.

Removing the prize does not stop the flood

curl ended its bug bounty in January 2026 after AI submissions drove its confirmed-vulnerability rate below 5%. It returned in March paying nothing at all. The confirmed rate recovered to 15–16% — and volume doubled again anyway.● When an attempt costs nothing, prize size stops governing entry. The scarce resource becomes adjudication, and a contest bills all of it to the buyer while paying for one submission in N.

The comparables are dead or absorbed

Gitcoin wound down its grants business by May 2025, citing unsustainable unit economics.● Replit Bounties shut down on 6 September 2025 with no announcement, redirecting users to a human freelance platform.● Topcoder was absorbed into Wipro, InnoCentive into Wazoku. 99designs now bans generative AI in some contests● — an incumbent suppressing zero-cost supply rather than absorbing it.

05 — Method

How to check this yourself

What a grade means

A — Settling
Individual payouts are externally confirmable, by transaction hash or published payout report.
C — Unverified
Inventory is live, but no released payout could be confirmed from outside the platform.
D — Payout risk, abandoned, or saturated
Failed payouts observed, listings stale by months, or competitor counts that drive the value of an attempt to a fraction of a cent.

Reading the marks

● Confirmed. Verified against settlement evidence: an on-chain transfer, a published payout report, or documented payout terms.

○ Claimed. The platform asserts it and we could not independently verify it. Usually a dashboard total.

— Not verifiable. The figure is not public. This is never recorded as zero; a measured zero is written as 0.

Standing limits

These are point-in-time observations of public data, captured 24 July 2026. Inventory moves daily and a grade can change with it.
